The No Contact Rule: The Complete Guide

By the maker of No Contact Tracker · Updated August 2026

The no contact rule means cutting off all communication with your ex after a breakup. No texts, no calls, no likes, no story views, no "I saw this and thought of you." It is the single most recommended piece of breakup advice for a reason: it works. Not instantly, and not painlessly, but it works.

This guide covers what no contact actually involves, why it is so hard, how long to do it, and what to do at 11pm when your thumb is hovering over their name.

Why no contact works

A breakup is not just sadness. Your brain built habits around another person: the good-morning text, the shared jokes, the person you told things to first. When that ends, every one of those habits fires into a void, and each firing hurts.

Contact resets the clock. Every text you send, every story you watch, feeds the part of your brain that is still waiting for them to come back. Distance is what lets that part quiet down. People describe week three or four as the first time they went a whole morning without thinking about their ex. That does not happen while you are still checking their profile.

There is also a self-respect layer. Each urge you ride out without acting on it is proof that you can. That proof compounds. It is the difference between hoping you will feel better someday and watching yourself get better one day at a time.

What counts as contact

People get creative about loopholes, so let's be precise. All of these count:

  • Texting or calling them, including "just to return their stuff" when the stuff could wait
  • Replying when they text you (more on exceptions below)
  • Liking, commenting, or reacting to anything they post
  • Watching their stories, checking when they were last online, scrolling their profile
  • Asking mutual friends about them, or posting things designed for them to see
  • Showing up at places you know they will be

The social media ones matter more than most people expect. Watching from a distance feels harmless because they never know. But your brain knows. Silent checking keeps the attachment loop open all by itself. Mute them, or unfollow if you can bear it, and block if you keep relapsing.

How long should no contact last?

30 days is the standard starting point, and it is a good one: long enough for real change to start, short enough to commit to. Many people extend to 60 or 90 once they see the difference.

The honest benchmark is not a number, though. It is this: no contact has done its job when hearing from them would not knock you over. If a text from them today would send you spiraling, you are not done. If it would land like a message from an old classmate, you are.

One warning from watching thousands of people do this: the third and fourth weeks are the quiet danger zone. The acute pain fades, you feel almost normal, and that is exactly when "one friendly text won't hurt" starts sounding reasonable. It is not weakness, it is how recovery feels from the inside. Expect it and plan for it.

The exceptions: kids, work, shared leases

If you co-parent, work together, or share a lease, full no contact is not possible, and pretending otherwise sets you up to fail. The version that works is often called limited contact:

  • Only about logistics: the kids, the schedule, the apartment
  • Brief, polite, and boring. No emotional check-ins, no "how have you been"
  • In writing where possible, so there is no late-night phone call energy

The rule underneath is the same: no emotional contact. You can hand over the keys without reopening the wound.

What to do instead of texting them

Willpower alone is a bad plan, because urges do not arrive when you are strong. They arrive at 11pm, after a drink, after a song, after a dream. You need replacements ready before the urge comes:

Ride the wave

An urge to text feels permanent but behaves like a wave: it builds, peaks, and passes, usually within 10 to 15 minutes. Your only job is to outlast one wave at a time. Set a timer for ten minutes and do literally anything else. When the timer ends, the urge is usually smaller.

Write it, don't send it

Everything you want to say to them, say it. In a note, in a journal, in an unsent letter. The words leave your body without landing in their inbox. People who write unsent letters early in a breakup tend to stick with no contact noticeably longer, and it makes sense: the pressure has somewhere to go.

Count the days

Track your streak somewhere you can see it. Day 9 sounds small until you remember day 2, and breaking a visible streak costs more than breaking an invisible one. This is the entire reason day counters work for quitting anything, and an ex is something you quit.

Tell one person

Pick one friend who knows you are doing this. When the urge hits, the deal is you text them first. Saying "I really want to text my ex right now" out loud to someone else takes about half the power out of it.

If you break no contact

Most people slip at least once. If you do, two things are true at the same time: the slip matters less than you think, and it teaches more than you think.

You do not lose your progress. Fourteen clean days followed by one text is not day zero of your healing, whatever the counter says. Your brain keeps what it learned. But do look honestly at what happened right before the slip. Was it a specific time of night? Alcohol? Their name in a playlist? A photo memory notification? That trigger will come back, and this time you can be ready for it.

Then restart the count and keep going. The people who make it through are not the ones who never slip, they are the ones who restart without turning one slip into a full relapse.

What no contact is not

It is not a silent treatment, and it is not a trick. If your whole plan is "ignore them until they miss me," you are still emotionally on call, checking your phone and reading meaning into their silence. That version keeps the wound open. Do it for yourself, and if they come back someday, you will be in a position to actually decide what you want, instead of grabbing the first hit of relief.

Frequently asked questions

What if they text me first?

You are not obligated to reply, and you are allowed to take a day to decide. If it is logistics, keep it brief and boring. If it is "I miss you" at midnight, remember that a message sent at their weakest moment is not a plan, it is a mood. Protect your streak first.

Does no contact work if we ended on good terms?

Yes, and it can matter even more. Friendly breakups leave the door cracked open, which keeps both people half-attached. You can be friends later, once being around them costs you nothing. Later usually means months, not weeks.

Should I tell my ex I'm going no contact?

One short message is fine if it gives you closure: "I need some space to heal, so I won't be in touch for a while. Wishing you well." Then actually go quiet. Announcing it repeatedly is contact.

Is it normal to feel worse before feeling better?

Completely. The first week of no contact often hurts more than the breakup itself, because you removed the painkiller too. That spike is withdrawal, and it passes. Most people feel the first real shift somewhere in weeks two to four.
