No Contact Apps, Honestly Compared

By the maker of No Contact Tracker · Updated August 2026

Full disclosure before anything else: we make one of the apps on this page. Most "best breakup app" articles are written by an app's own team and quietly rank themselves first. We would rather name the bias and then earn your trust by being specific about what each kind of app does well, including where ours is not the right pick.

Start with your failure mode, not with a ranking

Breakup apps look similar in screenshots and behave very differently at 2am. The honest way to choose is to name the thing that actually breaks your no contact:

  • You send impulsive texts at night. You need a visible streak and a fast SOS flow that stands between you and the keyboard.
  • You ruminate and want to talk it out. You need an AI coach or journaling prompts more than a counter.
  • You feel alone with it. You need other humans who get it, which no tracker provides.
  • You need someone to hand you a plan. A fixed day-by-day program beats an open-ended toolkit.

The types, and who each one fits

Day counter + SOS apps (this is what we build)

No Contact Tracker is built around one job: getting you through the urge without sending the text. A day counter you will not want to reset, a one-tap SOS flow (breathing, your own reasons, your own rules), mood check-ins that show you the curve, unsent letters for the words that need to leave your body, and an AI coach for the nights you need to talk. Journals and letters stay on your device.

Where it is not the best pick: there is no community, and if what you mainly want is long, open-ended AI conversation, an AI-first app will feel richer. We built for the moment of the urge, not for hour-long chats.

AI-first coach apps

Apps like NoContact AI and Breakup Buddy lead with the conversation: chat support, message analysis, guided reflection. This fits people who process by talking and want a responsive companion at any hour. Two things to check honestly: what happens to your chat logs (read the privacy policy, not the marketing page), and whether the app still helps in the ten seconds of a spike, when nobody wants to type paragraphs.

Community apps

Apps built around shared stories fit people whose main wound is loneliness. Reading someone on day 40 while you are on day 4 genuinely helps. The risk is the mirror image: a feed full of breakup stories can keep the breakup at the center of your day. If you notice yourself scrolling pain instead of processing yours, that is the signal to step back.

Fixed-program apps

30-day and 90-day programs (Rx Breakup and Detach90 are known examples) hand you a plan: today, do this. That structure is a gift when grief has flattened your decision-making. The tradeoff is rigidity. Grief is not linear, and on the day the program says "reflect on gratitude" while you are shaking with the urge to text, a fixed script has less to offer.

Whichever you pick, check these three things

  1. Privacy. A breakup journal is some of the most sensitive text you will ever write. Where is it stored? Are AI chats retained or used for training? Local-first is the strongest default.
  2. The 2am test. Open the app and count taps from launch to actual help. If real support is more than two taps deep, it will lose to your messages app in a spike.
  3. What it promises. An app that promises to get your ex back is optimizing for your hope, not your healing. Recovery tools should promise one thing: helping you not reopen the wound.

The honest bottom line

If your breakup is behaviorally hard (you know you should not text, and you do it anyway), a counter-plus-SOS app is the right category, and ours is a strong one that is free to start. If your breakup is mostly loneliness, choose community. If it is mostly rumination, choose an AI coach and read its privacy policy first. If it is decision paralysis, choose a program. Pricing and features change often, so check the current store listings before paying for anything, including ours.
